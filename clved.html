Reflection

A reflection on human dignity, conscience, freedom, forgiveness, compassion, moral transformation, and faithful Christian living through the meaningful experiences shown in our literary work.

CLVED REFLECTION

Seeing human dignity through stories.

Literature allows us to look beyond the events of a story and reflect on the values that guide our actions. Through our featured literary work, we are encouraged to examine how people treat one another and how prejudice, silence, and unfair judgment can affect a person's dignity.

The story also reminds us that Christian values are not only ideas we learn in class. They are values that should guide the way we listen, understand, forgive, show compassion, and stand up for what is right.

VALUES • REFLECTION • ACTION
SEVEN VALUES

A Reflection on What We Believe.

Each reflection connects the literary work with Christian teachings and encourages us to recognize our responsibility toward ourselves, others, and our community.

01 VALUE
First Reflection

Human Dignity

In Dekada '70 and in The Seat We Left Empty, human dignity is shown through the way people are affected when their worth is ignored or violated. In Dekada '70, Martial Law exposed how oppression and fear could affect the lives and freedoms of ordinary Filipino families. In our one-act play, Maya experiences a different form of injustice when others judge her based on her appearance and background. Both works remind us that every person has inherent worth because every human being is created in the image and likeness of God. Respecting another person means recognizing their value even before knowing their entire story.

02 VALUE
Second Reflection

Conscience

Conscience helps people recognize whether their choices are right or wrong. In Dekada '70, the characters are confronted with a society filled with fear, oppression, and injustice. Their experiences challenge them to question whether remaining silent is truly the right thing to do. In The Seat We Left Empty, Eli's conscience changes when he realizes that he judged Maya without knowing her story. Instead of ignoring his mistake, he admits that he was wrong. Both works show that conscience can encourage us to recognize injustice, correct our actions, and choose what is right.

03 VALUE
Third Reflection

Freedom and Responsibility

Freedom gives people the ability to make choices, but every choice also carries responsibility. In Dekada '70, the characters experience a time when freedom is heavily restricted, forcing them to think about what they can do in the face of oppression. In our one-act play, the students have the freedom to decide how they treat Maya, but they must also accept the consequences of their choices. The story reminds us that freedom should not be used to hurt, exclude, or judge others. Instead, it should be guided by responsibility, respect, and the common good.

04 VALUE
Fourth Reflection

Forgiveness

Forgiveness does not mean pretending that something painful never happened. It means choosing not to remain trapped by anger and resentment. In The Seat We Left Empty, Maya could have continued holding Eli's judgment against him. Instead, the characters choose to acknowledge what happened and learn from it. Eli also accepts his mistake instead of making excuses for his actions. This reflects the Christian call to forgive others while recognizing that forgiveness should also lead to change. Through forgiveness, people can begin rebuilding trust and creating healthier relationships.

05 VALUE
Fifth Reflection

Compassion

Compassion means being willing to understand another person's situation and respond with care. In Dekada '70, the experiences of the family reveal how difficult circumstances can deepen their concern for one another. In The Seat We Left Empty, Ana demonstrates compassion when she chooses to welcome Maya instead of judging her. Sam also becomes more understanding after learning about Maya's situation. These moments remind us that we do not always know what another person is experiencing. A compassionate Christian chooses to listen first and respond with kindness rather than immediately judging others.

06 VALUE
Sixth Reflection

Moral Transformation

Moral transformation happens when a person recognizes what needs to change and chooses to become better. In Dekada '70, the characters experience changes in how they understand oppression, family, and responsibility. In The Seat We Left Empty, Eli experiences a personal transformation after realizing that his assumptions caused Maya to feel excluded. He does not simply admit that he was wrong; he learns that respecting others requires him to question his own assumptions. This shows that transformation begins with self-awareness and becomes meaningful when it leads to better actions.

07 VALUE
Seventh Reflection

Faithful Christian Living

Both Dekada '70 and The Seat We Left Empty remind us that Christian living is reflected in the choices we make every day. Faith is not only expressed through words or prayer but also through how we treat people who may be different from us. We are called to protect human dignity, listen to our conscience, use freedom responsibly, forgive, show compassion, and stand against injustice. The stories encourage us to become people who do not simply notice unfairness but choose to respond with courage, kindness, and responsibility. By living these values, we can help create a community where every person feels respected, heard, and included.

OUR TAKEAWAY

Every person deserves to be seen, heard, and respected.

The stories we explored remind us that meaningful change can begin with something simple: choosing to listen before judging. As Christians, we are called to recognize the dignity of every person and allow our faith to guide the way we treat others.

Believe. Reflect. Live.

May these stories encourage us to become more compassionate, responsible, and courageous in the way we live out our Christian values.
