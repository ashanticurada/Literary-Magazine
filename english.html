An Original Multimodal One-Act Play

The Seat
We Left Empty

A Social Drama

Discrimination Prejudice Empathy Human Dignity
THE PLAY

Before we judge a story, should we hear it first?

Set in a public high school classroom in the Philippines, this one-act play follows a new student and the assumptions made about her before her classmates take the time to understand her.

Through five scenes, the story explores how ordinary assumptions can lead to exclusion—and how listening, empathy, and the willingness to change can make room for someone who was once left out.

CHARACTERS

The People in the Story

Maya Garcia

New Student

A quiet, intelligent, and hardworking student who recently transferred to the school. She comes from a financially struggling family but does not want others to pity her.

Eli San Juan

Student

A confident student who is quick to judge people based on appearances. He is not intentionally cruel, but he has grown accustomed to making assumptions.

Sam Castro

Student

Eli's close friend. More thoughtful than Eli, but often remains silent when something feels wrong.

Ana Flores

Student

An empathetic student who believes that people should be understood before they are judged.

Mr. Cruz

Teacher

A calm and observant teacher who encourages his students to question their assumptions.

PROLOGUE

The Empty Seat

THE EMPTY SEAT

The stage is dark. One chair is illuminated by a single spotlight. A school bell rings.

[The stage is dark. One chair is illuminated by a single spotlight. A school bell rings.]
Projection “WHAT DO YOU SEE BEFORE YOU SEE A PERSON?”
[Lights slowly rise.]
MR. CRUZ
Every classroom has a seat. Sometimes, that seat belongs to the smartest student. Sometimes, it belongs to the funniest. Sometimes, it belongs to someone who hardly speaks at all.
MR. CRUZ
But what happens when we decide what a person is like before we ever hear their story?
[Blackout.]
SCENE 1

The New Student

[Lights up. A classroom. Students are talking before class. MAYA GARCIA enters quietly, carrying an old school bag. She looks around for a seat.]
ANA FLORES
Hi! Are you new here?
MAYA GARCIA
Yes. Hello, nice to meet you, I’m Maya!
ANA FLORES
Nice to meet you too, I'm Ana! You can sit here.
[Ana points toward an empty seat. Maya smiles and sits.]
Visual Element — Empty Classroom Seat
ELI SAN JUAN
Are you sure that’s the new student?
SAM CASTRO
Yeah.
ELI SAN JUAN
She doesn’t look like she came from our school.
SAM CASTRO
What does that even mean?
ELI SAN JUAN
I don’t know. She just… doesn’t fit in.
[Maya hears them but pretends not to.]
ANA FLORES
Maya, don’t mind them.
MAYA GARCIA
No, it’s okay. I don’t really mind, heh.
ANA FLORES
Are you sure you're okay with it?
MAYA GARCIA
Don’t you worry, I’m used to it.
[Ana looks concerned.]
ELI SAN JUAN
Hey, Maya. Can I ask you a question?
MAYA GARCIA
Yes, of course.
ELI SAN JUAN
Did you transfer from one of those small schools in the province?
MAYA GARCIA
Yes po, why did you ask?
ELI SAN JUAN
I knew it.
SAM CASTRO
Hmm, know what?
ELI SAN JUAN
Oh, nothing po.
[Mr. Cruz enters.]
MR. CRUZ
Good morning, class.
[Everyone stands up and bows their head.]
CLASS
Good morning, Sir!
MR. CRUZ
We have a new classmate today. Maya, would you like to introduce yourself?
[Maya stands.]
MAYA GARCIA
Good morning everyone, I’m Maya. I transferred here because my family moved. I hope we can get along and make new moments together!
MR. CRUZ
Welcome, Maya.
[Maya sits.]
Visual Element — Classroom
ELI SAN JUAN
(quietly to Sam) She seems really nonchalant.
SAM CASTRO
Maybe she’s just nervous because it is her first day here.
ELI SAN JUAN
Or maybe she thinks she’s better than us.
SAM CASTRO
How’d you think about that from only one sentence she spoke?
ELI SAN JUAN
I’m just following my intuitions, you know?
[The classroom bell rings.]
SCENE 2

The Assumption

[The following week. Students are preparing for a group presentation.]
MR. CRUZ
Your task is to create a presentation about equality and inclusion. I will divide you into groups.
ELI SAN JUAN
Are you kidding me right now?
ANA FLORES
What’s the matter?
ELI SAN JUAN
Just nothing..
MAYA GARCIA
I can help work on the research.
ELI SAN JUAN
Hey, Maya. Maybe you can just do the easy part.
MAYA GARCIA
Uhm, excuse me. What do you mean?
ELI SAN JUAN
You’re just a newbie. You might not know how things work here.
ANA FLORES
She can decide what part she wants to do. She has the free will to decide on what to help.
ELI SAN JUAN
Ana, I’m just trying to make things easier.
MAYA GARCIA
I can handle the research.
ELI SAN JUAN
You know what, fine.
[Maya takes out a notebook.]
MAYA GARCIA
I already have some sources.
ELI SAN JUAN
Already?
MAYA GARCIA
Yes.
ELI SAN JUAN
How?
MAYA GARCIA
I read about the topic last night.
SAM CASTRO
You did the research already?
MAYA GARCIA
A little.
[Maya hands Ana her notes.]
ANA FLORES
These are really detailed.
ELI SAN JUAN
You actually did all this?
MAYA GARCIA
I said I could handle it.
[Eli becomes slightly uncomfortable.]
ELI SAN JUAN
Okay.
[He looks away.]
SAM CASTRO
You judged her pretty quickly.
ELI SAN JUAN
I didn’t judge her.
SAM CASTRO
You literally assumed she couldn’t do the work.
ELI SAN JUAN
I was wrong. So what?
ANA FLORES
That’s exactly what we’re supposed to be presenting about.
[Silence.]
SCENE 3

What They Didn’t Know

[After class. Maya remains behind to organize her notes. Sam enters.]
SAM CASTRO
Maya?
MAYA GARCIA
Yeah?
SAM CASTRO
Can I ask you something?
MAYA GARCIA
Sure.
SAM CASTRO
Why did you transfer?
[Maya pauses.]
MAYA GARCIA
My mother lost her job.
SAM CASTRO
Oh.
MAYA GARCIA
We had to move in with my grandmother for a while.
SAM CASTRO
I’m sorry.
MAYA GARCIA
It’s okay.
SAM CASTRO
I didn’t know.
MAYA GARCIA
You weren’t supposed to know.
[Eli enters and hears the last sentence.]
ELI SAN JUAN
Maya?
[Maya turns.]
ELI SAN JUAN
I… heard what happened.
MAYA GARCIA
It’s fine.
ELI SAN JUAN
No. It isn’t.
[Pause.]
ELI SAN JUAN
I thought you were quiet because you thought you were better than everyone.
MAYA GARCIA
And I thought you were mean because you hated me.
ELI SAN JUAN
Maybe I was afraid of not understanding you.
MAYA GARCIA
Then you should have asked.
[Eli looks down.]
ELI SAN JUAN
You’re right.
[Ana enters.]
ANA FLORES
What’s happening?
SAM CASTRO
We’re learning that assumptions are terrible storytellers.
ANA FLORES
That’s actually a good line.
MAYA GARCIA
Because assumptions tell a story without asking the person who lived it.
[Everyone becomes quiet.]

“Assumptions tell a story without asking the person who lived it.”

SCENE 4

The Presentation

[The classroom is arranged for presentations. The group stands in front.]
Projected Image Four silhouettes separated by walls.
MR. CRUZ
You may begin.
ANA FLORES
Our topic is equality.
SAM CASTRO
But before we define equality, we want to talk about something that often prevents it.
ELI SAN JUAN
Judgment.
MAYA GARCIA
Sometimes discrimination doesn’t begin with hatred.
[Pause.]
MAYA GARCIA
Sometimes it begins with an assumption.
Visual Transition The walls between the silhouettes disappear.
ELI SAN JUAN
We assume someone is poor because of their clothes.
SAM CASTRO
We assume someone is unintelligent because they came from a different school.
ANA FLORES
We assume someone is unfriendly because they’re quiet.
MAYA GARCIA
We assume we know someone’s story because we saw one small part of it.
MAYA GARCIA
But a person is always more than what we first see.
ELI SAN JUAN
I used to think that discrimination was always obvious.
SAM CASTRO
Sometimes it is.
ELI SAN JUAN
But sometimes it’s a small decision.
ANA FLORES
Who gets invited.
MAYA GARCIA
Who gets trusted.
SAM CASTRO
Who gets heard.
ELI SAN JUAN
And who gets left behind.
[The empty chair from the beginning is moved to the center of the stage.]
MAYA GARCIA
This chair represents the people we leave out because we think we already know who they are.
[Silence.]
MR. CRUZ
And what should we do when we realize we were wrong?
ELI SAN JUAN
Listen.
ANA FLORES
Apologize.
SAM CASTRO
Change.
MAYA GARCIA
And make room.
SCENE 5

The Empty Chair

[The students return to their seats. The empty chair remains in the center.]
MR. CRUZ
Class, I want you to remember something.
[He walks toward the empty chair.]
MR. CRUZ
Discrimination does not always begin with someone saying, “You don’t belong.” Sometimes it begins with a thought:

“She probably can’t do it.”

“He probably doesn’t belong here.”

“They’re probably different from us.”

MR. CRUZ
And if we never question those thoughts, they become decisions.
MAYA GARCIA
And decisions become exclusion.
MR. CRUZ
Exactly.
[Eli stands and walks toward the empty chair.]
ELI SAN JUAN
Can I say something?
MR. CRUZ
Go ahead.
ELI SAN JUAN
I thought I knew Maya.
[He looks at Maya.]
ELI SAN JUAN
I didn’t.
MAYA GARCIA
None of us knew everything about each other.
ELI SAN JUAN
But now I know that I don’t have to know someone’s whole story before I respect them.
[Maya smiles slightly.]
ANA FLORES
Maybe that’s the point.
SAM CASTRO
We don’t need to understand everything about someone to treat them with dignity.
Final Projection “EVERY PERSON HAS A STORY YOU HAVE NOT HEARD YET.”
MAYA GARCIA
Maybe we should move it.
ELI SAN JUAN
Why?
MAYA GARCIA
Because no one should have to sit alone.
[The students move the empty chair beside the others.]
[The lighting gradually becomes brighter.]
MR. CRUZ
A classroom becomes a community when everyone has a place in it.
[Bell rings. Nobody immediately leaves.]
ANA FLORES
Ready?
MAYA GARCIA
Ready.
[The students leave together. The stage remains empty except for the chairs, now arranged together.]
Final Projection “LOOK AGAIN. LISTEN FIRST. JUDGE LESS. MAKE ROOM.”
[Lights fade.]

END

The Seat We Left Empty is an original multimodal one-act play about the assumptions we make, the stories we fail to hear, and the importance of making room for others.
